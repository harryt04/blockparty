# Brand Strategy

> **Working premise:** This is a free, open-source web game for people of all ages that faithfully implements the accepted classic property-trading rules baseline while presenting it through Blockparty's original magical-city world, language, art direction, and table interface. It is not positioned as a replacement, sequel, edition, or unofficial version of any existing game.

Related documents: [Naming](naming.md) and [IP safety](../legal/ip-safety.md). Naming is provisional until the clearance and release gates in those documents are complete.

> **Provisional mark: Blockparty.** Adopted for product, design, and content work on 2026-09-02. Not cleared. **Civora** remains the designated fallback. See the decision record in [Naming](naming.md). Every design and content choice must survive a name change.

## Positioning

**For** friends, families, and online groups of all ages who want a quick-to-learn economic board game with real negotiation,
**Blockparty** is a free, open-source web property game that brings a familiar classic rules contract to an original magical city, turning planning, timing, and deals into a lively shared table.
Its independent identity comes from the Blockparty world, names, copy, art direction, interface, content provenance, and community practice—not from claiming a different rules genre.

### Audience and promise

| Audience                           | Need                                                        | Brand promise                                    |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Casual social players              | A friendly game night that does not require a long tutorial | “Invite people, make deals, laugh at reversals.” |
| Strategically curious players      | Meaningful choices beyond dice outcomes                     | “Read the table, manage risk, negotiate well.”   |
| Open-source hosts and contributors | A transparent, adaptable game they can run themselves       | “Play it, inspect it, and help shape it.”        |

The tone is **social and welcoming, but strategically sharp**. It should never talk down to new players or imply that success is pure luck.

## Brand principles

1. **Classic table, unmistakably new world.** Teach the accepted classic rules contract through Blockparty's original magical-city names, copy, assets, and presentation; never imply affiliation with another product.
2. **The table is the product.** Design language should invite bargaining, celebration, friendly rivalry, and shared stories.
3. **Strategy is legible.** Explain choices, costs, ownership, and consequences clearly; avoid false complexity.
4. **Open by default.** Describe the project honestly as free/open source, credit contributors, and make asset origins traceable.
5. **Originality is a feature.** Independent names, copy, assets, provenance, and presentation are product requirements, not a last-minute legal polish.
6. **All-ages friendliness.** Keep copy suitable for a general audience; avoid gambling framing, hostile trash talk, and manipulative urgency.

### Recommendation: Blockparty

Adopt **Blockparty** as the creative direction: an original magical city led by Moonquill Market, Giltglass Exchange, Starhold Academy, and Blackglass Keep. The four territories above stay on record as the alternatives considered; the accepted classic table is the product's rules and layout baseline, while the city supplies its independent identity.

The direction earns its place on three counts:

1. **It matches the product literally.** Players take a **seat**, accept an **invite**, and join a **host**. Those three terms are already canonical in the [Glossary](../product/glossary.md). A block party is what the software actually does.
2. **It carries negotiation naturally.** Magical-city districts, portals, and exchanges give deal-making a clear setting without borrowing a source product's expression.
3. **The world is original and expandable.** Moonquill Market, Giltglass Exchange, Starhold Academy, and Blackglass Keep give the team a coherent space to author districts, events, economic data, and art from scratch.

This is a product-fit judgment. It is not a claim of trademark, copyright, or trade-dress clearance.

#### Classic table guardrail

The accepted product baseline uses a 40-space perimeter table with four corner
anchors, eight Color Sets, four Transit Properties, two Utilities, two fee
spaces, and two 16-card decks. That composition is an intentional rules and
layout requirement, not a brand shortcut. The independent identity must come
from the magical-city names, original copy, independently created assets, table
materials, typography, and interface hierarchy recorded in [IP safety](../legal/ip-safety.md).

#### Tone guard

A block party is warm, not chaotic. Keep the strategic register from the Voice section below. The setting must not push the product toward whimsy that hides the economic decisions, and it must not drift toward a street-fair carnival with prize-and-raffle framing.

## Voice

### Voice attributes

| Attribute             | Sounds like                                             | Does not sound like                          |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Inviting              | “Bring a few friends. The town is open.”                | “Dominate your opponents.”                   |
| Clear                 | “This district earns when visitors arrive.”             | Dense financial jargon or unexplained slang. |
| Wry, not cruel        | “That was an ambitious bid.”                            | Humiliating loss messages or taunts.         |
| Strategically precise | “Your next upgrade raises recurring income and upkeep.” | “Just roll and hope.”                        |
| Community-minded      | “Host a room, suggest a rule set, contribute an icon.”  | Ownership claims that erase contributors.    |

### Copy examples

- **Welcome:** “The street’s closed. Pull up a chair.”
- **Invite:** “Send the link. We’ll save them a seat.”
- **Waiting for players:** “Two seats still open. The music can wait.”
- **Negotiation prompt:** “Someone’s offering a trade. Compare the long game before you say yes.”
- **Acquisition:** “4 Maple Stoop is yours. Rent starts the next time a guest walks past.”
- **Setback:** “A neighbor called it in. Sit out a turn — your Block keeps earning.”
- **Late game:** “Two Blocks left unclaimed. This is where the evening turns.”
- **Open-source note:** “Free to play, open to inspect, built with credited contributions.”

### Do / don’t

**Do**

- Describe actions in plain, original language: acquire, negotiate, improve, collect, preserve reserves.
- Lead with shared play and meaningful choices.
- Credit artists, font makers, code contributors, and source licenses where required.
- Say “in the property-trading board-game genre” when category context is useful.

**Don’t**

- Use another game’s title, slogans, character names, space names, rule copy, or recognizable taglines in public copy.
- Call the project “the open-source version,” “clone,” “remake,” “compatible with,” or “like [brand]” in marketing, metadata, screenshots, or app-store copy.
- Copy another product’s names, wording, illustrations, scans, token artwork,
  card copy, or distinctive interface assets. Familiarity with the accepted
  rules baseline is not permission to copy source expression.
- Promise legal status, availability, or safety before actual review.

## Visual system guardrails

### Proposed Blockparty palette and form language

The setting is an original magical city at a shared evening table. The cream
board, dark ink, tactile cards, unmistakable Color Set colors, and restrained
material cues are the brand direction; the detailed token and component rules
belong to the [Design System](../design/design-system.md).

- **Base:** warm board cream, dark ink, and a restrained evening navy for dark mode.
- **Brand accent:** a single warm signal color for the current turn and primary action.
- **Property groups:** eight unmistakable group colors, always paired with labels,
  patterns, or icons so color is never the sole state indicator.
- **Players:** six original silhouettes with distinct colors and patterns.
- **Materials:** tactile cards, paper edges, and table marks that support hierarchy
  without copying a familiar card or board treatment.
- **Type:** a licensed, readable contemporary sans paired with a restrained editorial serif only after license review. Hand-lettered display faces are tempting here; verify the license and the legibility at 200% zoom before committing.
- **Motion:** short, purposeful transitions that explain movement, deal status, and economic changes; honor reduced-motion preferences.

#### Motifs to avoid

Beyond the standing prohibitions in [IP safety](../legal/ip-safety.md), this setting has its own traps. Avoid alcohol cues, prize wheels, raffle tickets, and cash-toss imagery. The general-audience brand bans gambling framing. Avoid block-shaped or chain-link iconography that reads blockchain.

### Familiar without confusing source

Familiarity should come from the accepted classic rules contract: turn-taking,
acquiring and improving Properties, Rent, resource pressure, and negotiation.
The independent identity comes from the magical-city content, names, copy,
assets, and interface. Test the complete product with a “source confusion”
review: a player should understand the rules baseline, but should not reasonably
believe it is affiliated with, endorsed by, or derived from a specific commercial
game.

## Provisional terminology

All terms below are working language, not a clearance result. Do not reuse external board labels, card headings, currency names, character names, or flavor text.

### Two layers, one mapping

The project runs **two vocabularies**, and they must not be confused.

- The **wire layer** is the canonical engineering vocabulary in the [Glossary](../product/glossary.md): `deed`, `district`, `transit`, `utility`, `detention`, `rest`, `start`, `landmark`, `improvement`, `bank`. It appears in commands, domain events, wire fields, and tests. **Blockparty does not change it.** A name change must never trigger a schema migration.
- The **display layer** is what a player reads. It is defined here and mapped one-to-one in the Glossary.

| Wire term              | Blockparty display name  | Notes                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| board / route          | Board                    | The 40-space classic table defined by the versioned content bundle.                |
| deed                   | Property                 | One ownable spot named by the magical-city content bundle.                         |
| district               | Color Set                | Complete related Properties that permit improvements.                              |
| improvement            | House or Hotel           | The two improvement kinds in the classic rules baseline.                           |
| landmark               | Hotel                    | The final improvement level; canonical wire vocabulary remains stable.              |
| transit                | Transit Property         | A non-district Property with count-based Rent.                                     |
| utility                | Utility                  | A non-district Property with dice-based Rent.                                      |
| bank                   | Bank                     | The non-player counterparty.                                                        |
| Start                  | Start                    | The route origin with its content-defined crossing payment.                        |
| Rest                   | Rest                     | Neutral space with no standard effect.                                             |
| Detention              | Detention                | Constrained player state and its board location.                                   |
| Detention-release card | Detention release card   | A held card that releases a player from Detention.                                 |
| fee space              | Fee                      | A content-defined payment space.                                                   |
| variant jackpot pool   | Pot                      | Variant-only shared pool; never the standard rule.                                 |
| event decks            | Event cards              | Two separately identified, original decks and their content.                       |
| currency               | Crown                    | The content-defined integer minor-unit currency.                                   |
| player pieces          | Pieces                   | The six original selectable pieces defined by the content bundle.                  |
| obligation             | Debt                     | What a player must pay, with the reason shown.                                      |
| bankruptcy             | Bankruptcy               | Elimination under the canonical rules.                                             |
| redeem mortgage        | Redeem Mortgage          | Clearing a mortgaged Property.                                                     |
| host capability        | Host controls            | The host's lobby and recovery controls.                                            |
| seat, invite, host     | unchanged                | Already block-party words. Same term in both layers.                               |

**Rule.** A display name is presentation only. Never introduce one into a command, event, wire field, database column, or test fixture. Never let a display name become the source of truth for game state.

## Asset provenance and production rules

Every public asset needs a provenance record before merge: creator, creation date, repository path, license/assignment, source files, modification history, and approval. Keep research references separate from production assets.

- Commission or create the magical-city districts, UI, copy, sound, animations,
  icons, token shapes, and presentation assets independently; record the
  accepted classic rules/economy baseline as versioned product content.
- Never trace, recolor, crop, prompt-copy, or “clean up” a third party’s board, cards, art, text, or audio.
- For generative tools, retain prompts, tool/version, source inputs, output selection rationale, and human modification record; do not use recognizable third-party inputs or style imitation requests.
- Maintain `NOTICE`/attribution requirements for third-party assets and verify font, icon, audio, and code licenses.
- Treat a missing record as a blocked asset, not an implicit approval.

For the detailed intake, licensing split, and release gate, follow [IP safety](../legal/ip-safety.md). For product-name research and decision records, follow [Naming](naming.md).

## Operating checklist

Before publishing a screen, trailer, social post, store listing, or release:

1. Check names and descriptors against the approved naming decision.
2. Run the source-confusion review on copy, screenshots, board geometry, and metadata.
3. Confirm each visible/audible asset has provenance and a compatible license.
4. Remove research-only references from public materials.
5. Route material brand changes through the attorney release gate described in [IP safety](../legal/ip-safety.md).
